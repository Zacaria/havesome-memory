▰ MEMORY ENGINEERING THE BEGINNING

A FICTIONAL COMPANY. A FAMILIAR PROBLEM.

Meet Morrow Works.

The story starts here ↓

FIRST, THE PLACE

Rooms to book.
Equipment to rent.

Morrow Works runs creative workspaces in France and Germany.

People book rooms, borrow recording gear, change their plans, and ask staff what the rules are.

01 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

What they know is spread across files.

A handbook, meeting notes, a copy pasted into the helpdesk. Each file holds part of the answer.

Your job is to make it usable, for the staff and for the AI assistant they work with.

We’ll follow one member’s question from start to finish. No background needed.

02 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

A member asks a simple question.

“How much notice makes cancelling my room booking free?”

The room is in France. They booked it on 4 May 2027. Today is 8 May, and we want the general rule as the member would see it.

Those details matter. Rules differ by country, by product and by booking date.

03 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

Search the file names. Nothing.

None of the five file names contains “cancellation”. The decision we need is saved as notes-04-18.md.

The answer exists. We were looking in the wrong place.

Searching file names works fine when you already know which file you want.

04 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

Search inside the files, and four answer.

Now the decision shows up, and it says 7 days. So do the original terms, the handbook and the helpdesk copy. They all say 14.

Finding passages like this is called retrieval. It found the statements. It can’t tell you which one applies.

This search really runs, in your browser, over the fictional files.

05 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

Three copies, one decision.

The handbook copied the original terms, and the helpdesk copied the handbook. All three 14s come from one place.

The 7 comes from an approved board decision. A newer-looking file name doesn’t overrule it, and neither does a majority.

What matters is where each statement came from, not how often it appears.

06 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

Does the change cover this booking?

The board approved it on 18 April. It covers France room bookings made from 1 May. The team’s files received it on 3 May.

Our booking was made on 4 May and we’re asking on 8 May, so it’s covered. The answer is 7 days.

An April booking in France, or any booking in Germany, would get a different answer.

07 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

The assistant needs the reasons, not just the number.

An AI assistant only sees what you hand it for this answer. That bundle is called its context.

Here we hand it the rule for which documents win, plus the decision that applies. A document left out of the bundle can’t help, however well it was stored.

08 / FOLLOW ONE QUESTION

Saving the file was the easy part.

We had to find it, work out how it related to the others, check when it applied, and hand over the right part.

When a rule changes tomorrow, all of that has to be redone. That work, more than storage, is what we mean by memory.

We started with a known question. Teams also need to explore files before they know what to ask. That’s next.

CHAPTER TWO / FINDING YOUR WAY

One question answered.
The rest of the files still unexplored.

Keeping documents wasn’t enough. Now let’s use the files when we don’t yet know what to search for.

01 / FINDING YOUR WAY

The team wants the bigger picture.

The member has an answer. Now the team wants to understand what happens when bookings change.

An operations note is a start: rooms sit empty after cancellations, while other requests come in too late. Could those freed rooms go to someone else?

Still 8 May, still the member’s view. The note records what staff noticed. Nobody measured anything.

02 / FINDING YOUR WAY

One note, several ways in.

File the note under “operations” and it’s stored. But someone studying booking changes won’t think to look there.

Tag it three ways instead: room capacity, booking lifecycle, preparation work. It’s still one note, reachable from three directions.

Start with rough categories you can change later. You don’t need a perfect filing system first.

03 / FINDING YOUR WAY

Two things called Atlas.

“Atlas” shows up twice in the files. One is a room in France. The other is a recording kit in Germany.

The shared name can put them in the same search results. It must not merge them, or apply the room’s rules to the kit.

Grouping asks “what is this related to?” Identity asks “which thing is this?” Keep the two apart.

04 / FINDING YOUR WAY

A promising link still needs evidence.

Back to the empty-rooms note. A separate draft proposes a two-day cancellation window for a trial in French rooms. It also asks someone to compare rebooking and preparation costs.

Read together, the two give the team something to investigate. They don’t show the trial would fill rooms, and the draft is still only a draft.

Keep the link and the doubt together. Nothing in these files measures what a shorter window would do.

05 / FINDING YOUR WAY

A good summary keeps the doubt in.

The team writes a short overview: empty rooms were noticed, a trial was proposed, rebooking was never measured.

Squash that into “shorter notice fills rooms” and the doubt disappears. Keep what was seen, what was proposed and what’s missing on separate lines, each linked to its source.

A summary is one more thing to check and correct. It doesn’t replace the files.

06 / FINDING YOUR WAY

The files suggest the next question.

“Do freed rooms actually get booked again?” Nobody asked that at first. It came from reading the notes side by side.

Answering it needs booking data and a way to test the trial. These files can’t give a rate or prove a cause. Say so, and don’t make up a number.

You went from finding a known answer to finding what you still need to learn. A clear gap is a useful result.

CHAPTER THREE / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

The rule changed.
Did the memory?

The team can find its way around. Keeping that knowledge right means following each change past the document that announced it.

Let’s rewind to when the 7-day decision first arrived.

01 / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

The rule changed before the files caught up.

Rewind to 3 May 2027. The board minutes with the 7-day rule have just reached the team’s files. The board approved the rule on 18 April, and it took effect on 1 May.

Approved, in effect, received: three different dates. Arriving late doesn’t move the date the rule took effect.

We’re still looking back from 8 May as a member. No new decision is invented here.

02 / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

A new rule doesn’t replace every old one.

The decision sets 7 days for France room bookings made on or after 1 May. France room bookings made from 1 January 2027 up to 30 April keep 14 days.

Rooms in Germany and equipment rentals don’t change. The update has to land on the right rule, not overwrite every cancellation rule in the files.

Examples here are about ATLAS-ROOM-FR, seen by a member on 8 May. They cover the general terms, not individual waivers.

03 / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

The source is current. The summary isn’t.

Say the team saved a one-line summary on 2 May: “France room bookings need 14 days’ notice.” It matched the old terms and missed a change already in effect.

Then the minutes arrive. The summary doesn’t fix itself because a new file showed up. Staff, or the assistant, could still be handed the old line.

The decision itself is still S03.

04 / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

Fix the summary. Keep the old one on record.

The new summary splits the cases: 14 days for France room bookings made from 1 January 2027 up to 30 April, 7 days from 1 May. It links to the original terms, the change and the rules for when each applies.

The old summary can stay in the history, marked incomplete. It must not be served as today’s answer.

Keeping an old version shows what the system believed. It doesn’t show that the belief was right.

05 / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

Two questions about the past, two answers.

“What applies to a France room booked on 29 April?” That’s about the booking’s terms. Seen from 8 May, it’s still 14 days, because the booking came before the change.

“What did the files contain on 2 May?” That’s about the files. They didn’t have S03 yet. That doesn’t mean the change wasn’t in effect.

Both are about ATLAS-ROOM-FR and the member’s view. The first uses what’s known on 8 May. The second rebuilds what the files held on 2 May.

06 / WHEN KNOWLEDGE CHANGES

Some conflicts have no winner.

Back on 8 May, the team finds another problem, this time for ATLAS-KIT-DE, the recording kit in Germany. For returns from 6 May, one logistics lead says “before 17:00 UTC”. Another says “before 18:00 UTC”.

Both were written on 4 May, both leads have the same authority, and neither cancels the other. Which paragraph comes first doesn’t settle it.

Keep both, mark the deadline unresolved, and ask logistics for a ruling. A tidy merged answer would hide the conflict.

This is a return-time question, not a cancellation rule. The files never settle it.

CHAPTER FOUR / TRUST AND CONTROL

Knowing isn’t permission.

The team can track changes and keep doubt visible. Now its files serve people with different access, and an assistant that only sees what it’s handed.

Back to the member desk, with one booking number.

01 / TRUST AND CONTROL

A booking number isn’t a key.

8 May 2027. Someone asks about DEMO-R17, a France room booked on 29 April. We can explain the general cancellation terms. Can we also say what this booking will cost?

Not from the member’s side. Waiver records are for finance only. Knowing the booking number gives no access, and the answer mustn’t hint at whether a waiver exists.

So the answer stays general: here is the policy, and here is who can check your booking. A file being stored somewhere doesn’t mean everyone may use it.

Member · ATLAS-ROOM-FR · DEMO-R17 · booked 29 April 2027 · as of 8 May.

02 / TRUST AND CONTROL

Search found the right rule. The assistant never saw it.

A different booking now: the Atlas room in France, booked on 4 May 2027. On 8 May the member asks for the general free-cancellation notice, not the fee for one cancellation.

Search finds both the original terms and the change. But the app passes only the original 14-day passage to the assistant. The change never reaches it.

Context is what the assistant is given for this one answer. Finding evidence and putting it into context are two steps, and things can go wrong between them.

The 4 May booking falls under S03’s France-room rule, and the member may read both sources.

03 / TRUST AND CONTROL

Send the scope along with the evidence.

The team fixes the bundle. It now says which room, country, product, booking date, reader and knowledge date it’s about, alongside the rules for when each policy applies and the policy passages themselves.

For this France room booked on 4 May, the change gives 7 days. The original terms and the change both stay visible, so the number arrives with its reasons.

This doesn’t mean sending every file. Pick what the reader may see and what’s relevant, plus what’s needed to read it correctly. More text won’t make up for missing evidence.

Same case: member · ATLAS-ROOM-FR · booked 4 May 2027 · as of 8 May.

04 / TRUST AND CONTROL

The right source can still be misread.

Even with the fixed bundle, an assistant could treat 7 days as a rule for everything, and wrongly apply it to rooms in Germany, equipment and older France bookings.

A good answer here is: “For this France room booked on 4 May 2027, free cancellation needs at least 7 days’ notice.” It doesn’t calculate a fee or reveal private exceptions.

The whole chain is now visible: stored, found, applicable, allowed, handed over, used correctly. Check each step. Passing one doesn’t mean the next will pass.

S03 leaves Germany rooms and equipment unchanged; S02 and S03 cover older France bookings. If a detail is missing, ask. Don’t guess.

05 / TRUST AND CONTROL

Three days later: which door?

11 May 2027. The same member is arriving at ATLAS-ROOM-FR. A notice received on 9 May says to use the courtyard entrance for arrivals from 10 to 12 May, during maintenance.

For an 11 May arrival, the notice is current. Its end date matters as much as what it says: it expires at 00:00 UTC on 13 May.

The notice has also been copied into an extracted fact, a summary and an answer cache. Which of those copies depend on it?

New date, new task: member · ATLAS-ROOM-FR · arriving 11 May 2027. The 8 May scenes couldn’t see this notice (S10).

06 / TRUST AND CONTROL

13 May: yesterday’s notice no longer answers.

At 00:00 UTC on 13 May the courtyard notice expires. For someone arriving that day, it can’t answer “which door?”. The files don’t say which entrance to use now, so don’t make one up.

The old notice can still explain what happened on 11 May. Useful as history and valid today are different questions.

Expired isn’t deleted. The records rule allows a short history window. Then the notice and every copy the team owns must be deleted at 00:00 UTC on 15 May.

Member · ATLAS-ROOM-FR · as of 13 May 2027. The two-day history window is this fictional company’s rule, not a general standard.

07 / TRUST AND CONTROL

15 May: deleting means the copies too.

At 00:00 UTC on 15 May the deletion has to reach four places the team owns: the source store, the extracted facts, the summary and the answer cache. Deleting only the original leaves the content behind elsewhere.

What remains is a receipt with the source ID and deletion time, without the notice itself. From these stores, nobody can now say which door the 11 May guest used.

Policy decisions and waiver history are outside this deletion rule.

Member · ATLAS-ROOM-FR · as of 15 May 2027. The rule covers stores the team owns.

END OF CHAPTER FOUR

Ask where the responsibility lives.

Who enforces access? Who chooses context? Who checks the answer? Who follows a correction or deletion through the copies?

These are now concrete questions to take to a system—not checkmarks to accept on a feature page.

S01 / Morrow Works / operating charter

S01#company

Morrow Works is a fictional creative-workspace company operating bookable rooms and recording-equipment rentals in France (FR) and Germany (DE). All organizations, identifiers, events and figures in this corpus are invented teaching data. No employee, client or personal data is represented.

S01#authority

Commercial terms are set by an approved policy-board decision. Published handbooks and helpdesk copies communicate those decisions; a newer publication date alone does not amend them. Proposals and operational observations are not commercial policy. Approved booking-specific waivers may override the general fee only within their stated booking and validity scope. Logistics leads may set equipment handover instructions; two equally authoritative instructions without a supersession relationship remain unresolved.

S01#applicability

Country means the service location, not the language of the question or the customer. Rooms and equipment are different products. Apply the commercial rule selected by the booking creation date: a later change applies only to bookings within its stated scope. Compare cancellation time against service start. In this teaching dataset timestamps use UTC and one notice day is exactly 24 hours; the threshold is inclusive. This is an exercise convention, not a claim about real contracts or local calendar law.

S01#readers

A member may read common company sources. Finance may also read booking-waiver records. These roles are simulated in a public teaching corpus, not real authorization. Knowing a booking identifier does not grant access to a finance record. A member answer may state the general policy and recommend an authorized booking-specific check; it must not reveal whether a hidden waiver exists.

S01#limits

The collection is a sample, not a complete company model. Absence from it does not establish that a rule or fact does not exist elsewhere. Preserve sources and distinguish extracted statements from inferred organization or explanations.

S02 / Original commercial terms

S02#rooms-fr

France / room bookings: cancellation is free with at least 14 notice days. Below 14 days, the cancellation fee is 20% of the booking price. Applies to bookings created from 1 January 2027, unless a later approved change explicitly applies.

S02#rooms-de

Germany / room bookings: cancellation is free with at least 14 notice days. Below 14 days, the cancellation fee is 30% of the booking price. Applies to bookings created from 1 January 2027, unless a later approved change explicitly applies.

S02#equipment

France and Germany / recording equipment: cancellation is free with at least 24 hours of notice. Below 24 hours, the cancellation fee is 50% of the rental price. Room-policy changes do not change equipment terms.

S02#boundaries

These terms concern cancellation only. They do not establish rescheduling fees, damage cover, deposits or late-return penalties.

S02#germany-history

Historical Germany revision: the policy board approved a reduction from 21 to 14 notice days on 1 January 2027, applying only to Germany room bookings created from that date. Germany room bookings created before 1 January 2027 retain free cancellation with at least 21 notice days; below that threshold the cancellation fee is 30%. This retained history is evidence recorded in January, not a source the collection possessed before its ingestion.

S03 / Policy-board decision / room flexibility

S03#decision

APPROVED. For France room bookings created on or after 1 May 2027, cancellation is free with at least 7 notice days. Below 7 days, the cancellation fee remains 20%. This supersedes the 14-day threshold in the original commercial terms only for these bookings. Earlier bookings retain their original terms. Germany rooms and all equipment rentals are unchanged.

S03#reason

The board used the operational note on unused room slots as one input to this decision. It proposes testing whether a shorter cancellation window helps room reuse. The note does not prove that this policy will increase occupancy or revenue.

S03#publication

The decision is approved on 18 April and takes effect for eligible bookings on 1 May. Handbook synchronization is not a condition of effectiveness. The knowledge collection receives these minutes on 3 May, after the effective date.

S07 / Unused slots and preparation work

S07#observations

Operations notes describe rooms left unused after cancellations, requests arriving close to the desired booking date, and preparation work that differs between a room booking and an equipment rental. These are qualitative observations, not an occupancy study or measured effect size.

S07#hypothesis

Working hypothesis: giving a room slot back sooner could allow reuse. It is not established how often released slots are rebooked, whether a shorter free-cancellation window changes behavior, or whether any revenue gain would exceed preparation costs.

S07#organization

Useful views include room capacity, booking lifecycle, equipment preparation and policy experiments. A single folder label such as operations does not express all of those connections.

S08 / Vocabulary and identity notes

S08#aliases

In these sources, cancel a room, release a reserved slot and void a reservation can refer to ending a booking. Move a booking means rescheduling and must not be silently treated as cancellation. This is a partial vocabulary note, not a complete taxonomy.

S11#delete

Expiration and deletion are separate. Between 13 and 15 May, the instruction may explain historical arrivals but must not answer current-entry questions. After deletion, the modeled system cannot answer the historical gate question from its owned stores. Commercial policy decisions and waiver history are not deleted by this rule.
